AI Vendor Comparison Scorecard

Comparing two AI tools or vendors? Score them across 10 criteria and get a data-driven recommendation. Built for consultant-client decision meetings — print or share the results.

Criteria (score 1-10)

What this is for

Software decisions get made on demo quality and whoever argued hardest, then justified afterwards. A scorecard does not remove judgement — it makes the judgement visible, which is what lets a group disagree productively instead of circularly. Its real value is that it surfaces where two people differ, which is usually one or two criteria rather than the overall verdict.

The ten criteria, and the three that decide it

All ten matter, but in practice three do most of the work for small and mid-sized buyers:

  • Ease of use for non-technical staff. The single best predictor of whether it is still in use next year. A more powerful tool nobody opens loses to a simpler one people actually run.
  • Integration with your existing tools. Where budget and timeline actually go. A tool that does not connect to where your work already lives creates a new silo instead of removing one.
  • Contract flexibility and exit options. The criterion buyers weight lowest and regret most. Ask directly: can you export your data, and what does month thirteen cost?

Score independently, then compare. Have each person score alone before anyone shares numbers. Scoring as a group produces consensus around whoever spoke first, which is the exact failure the scorecard exists to prevent.

Reading a close result

If the totals land within a few points, the tools are genuinely comparable and the scorecard has done its job — it has told you this is not where your attention should go. Decide on the criterion you care about most, or on price, and move on. Spending another three weeks on a decision the scorecard says is close is a more expensive mistake than picking the marginally worse tool.

If one wins decisively but you feel uneasy, the scorecard has surfaced something worth examining. Usually it means a criterion that matters to you is missing from the list, or one is worth far more than the others in your situation. Both are useful discoveries and neither is a reason to distrust the exercise.

Before you sign

Take the winner through the risk assessment, paying particular attention to vendor dependency and cost overrun — the two risks a scorecard comparison tends to under-weight because they only materialise after you have committed.
